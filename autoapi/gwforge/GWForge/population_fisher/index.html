gwforge.GWForge.population_fisher¶

Population (hyper-parameter) Fisher forecasting for GWForge.

Where GWForge.fisher asks how well a network measures one source, this asks how well it measures the population: the shape of the mass function, the history of the merger rate, the spin distribution, and – through spectral sirens – the cosmology.

The estimator is the first term of the hyper-parameter Fisher expansion of Gair, Antonelli & Barbieri (arXiv:2205.07893 Eq. 21),

\[\Gamma^{ij} = \sum_{k\ \rm detected} \big(s_k^i - \langle s\rangle^i\big)\big(s_k^j - \langle s\rangle^j\big), \qquad s_k^i = \frac{\partial \ln p(\theta_k \mid \Lambda)}{\partial \Lambda^i},\]

which is the small-measurement-error limit. The mean subtraction is the selection term, so there is no separate detection integral to evaluate and no rate normalisation to get right – see GWForge.population_fisher.population_fisher_term_I.

Pipeline¶

gwforge_population        --config-file bgp-gwtc5.ini --output-file population.h5
gwforge_optimal_snr       --injection-file population.h5 --output-file snr.h5 --ifos CE40 CE20 ET
gwforge_population_fisher --config-file mass_redshift.ini

Models¶

All four live in GWForge.population_fisher.model. The densities are GWForge’s own, called directly rather than reimplemented: GWForge.population._smoothed_mass for the BGP mass model, GWForge.population.redshift.madau_dickinson_psi_of_z() for the merger rate, and GWForge.population.spin.truncated_normal() for both halves of the Default spin model – so a forecast and the catalogue it runs on describe the same population by construction.

Derivatives¶

Analytic, everywhere, cosmology included – see GWForge.population_fisher.base for the two-tier policy and GWForge.cosmology for why differentiating under the integral beats finite-differencing the integral. Finite differences are the test oracle and the fallback for a model added later, not a production path; there is no autodiff tier and no JAX dependency.

The cumulative secondary-mass integral and its derivatives.

\[G(M) = \int_{m_{\min}}^{M} m_2^{\beta}\, S(m_2)\, dm_2,\]

so that \(C(m_1) = m_1^{-(\beta+1)} G(m_1)\) is the mass-ratio normalisation. Substituting \(m_2 = m_1 q\) turns one integral per event into one cumulative integral on the shared grid, interpolated – which is also how _smoothed_mass._norm_p_q does it, so the two agree.

dlog_prob_dmasses(events, parameters)[source]¶

(d ln p / d m_1, d ln p / d m_2) at fixed hyper-parameters.

Not needed by the Fisher itself – these are derivatives with respect to the event parameters, not the hyper-parameters. They are needed by GWForge.population_fisher.model, where a trial cosmology moves the redshift assigned to a measured distance and so moves the source-frame masses: \(\partial_\Lambda m^{\rm src} = -m^{\rm src}/(1+z)\cdot\partial_\Lambda z\).

\[\frac{\partial \ln p}{\partial m_1} = \frac{\partial \ln(\text{mixture})}{\partial m_1} + \frac{\partial \ln S(m_1)}{\partial m_1} - \frac{m_1^{\beta} S(m_1)}{G(m_1)},\]

the last term because \(m_1\) is the upper limit of the mass-ratio normalisation \(G\), and

\[\frac{\partial \ln p}{\partial m_2} = \frac{\beta}{m_2} + \frac{\partial \ln S(m_2)}{\partial m_2}.\]
Returns:

(d ln p / d m_1, d ln p / d m_2), nan outside the support.

Return type:

tuple of numpy.ndarray

analytic_score(events, parameters)[source]¶

Closed-form score, (n_events, 11).

Each column is the log-derivative of the numerator minus that of the normalisation, \(\partial_\Lambda \ln(\text{numerator}) - \partial_\Lambda \ln Z\), for the primary mass, plus the mass-ratio contribution for the two parameters (beta and delta_m) that appear in both. \(\partial_\Lambda Z\) is the trapezoid of \(\partial_\Lambda(\text{numerator})\) on the same nodes that normalise log_prob, which is exact because the nodes do not move.

log_shape(redshift, parameters)[source]¶

ln psi(z): the Madau-Dickinson shape alone, without the measure.

Public because GWForge.population_fisher.model rebuilds the measure at a trial cosmology and needs the shape on its own.

Includes the \([1 + (1+z_p)^{-\kappa}]\) factor that sets \(\psi(0) = 1\). That factor is independent of \(z\), so it multiplies the numerator and the normalisation alike and cancels from log_prob identically – which is why shape_gradient() omits it.

shape_gradient(redshift, parameters)[source]¶

d ln psi / dLambda, shape (n, 3).

Public for the same reason as log_shape().

With \(u = (1+z)/(1+z_p)\) and \(r = u^\kappa\), \(f = r/(1+r)\):

\[\partial_\gamma = \ln(1+z), \quad \partial_\kappa = -f \ln u, \quad \partial_{z_p} = \frac{\kappa f}{1 + z_p}.\]

classmethod from_npz(path)[source]¶

Reload a forecast that bin/gwforge_population_fisher saved.

Comparing two networks, or redrawing a figure in a new style, should not mean recomputing either forecast. The saved archive carries everything a plot needs: the matrix, its covariance, the parameter names, the fiducial values and the event counts.

The score matrix is not saved – it is one row per detected event and would dominate the file – so the reloaded result has an empty scores and mean_score. Everything the plotting path touches is present.

Parameters:

path (str) – A .npz written by the CLI.

Return type:

PopulationFisherResult

scaled_to(n_events)[source]¶

This forecast rescaled to a different number of detected events.

The Term-I Fisher is a sum over events, so it is proportional to \(N_{\rm det}\) in expectation and \(\sigma \propto N_{\rm det}^{-1/2}\). Use this to turn a one-month catalogue into a ten-year forecast without regenerating anything – or, more usefully, to state plainly that a headline number is a pure \(\sqrt{T}\) extrapolation rather than a computed one.

Invert a Fisher matrix, falling back to a pseudo-inverse when it is singular.

The matrix is normalised by its diagonal before inversion – the same trick GWForge.fisher.matrix.covariance() uses – because hyper-parameters differ by many orders of magnitude in scale (H0 near 68, lam_1 near 0.05) and the raw condition number is then dominated by units rather than by degeneracy.
